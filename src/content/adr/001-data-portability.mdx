## Context

Match Quality v2 needs richer profile signals: profession, schools, books and interests. The first idea was to pull them from the social platforms people already use: LinkedIn for career and education, Instagram and X for interests, Goodreads for books.

Before writing requirements we checked what each platform actually allows a consumer app to read:

- **LinkedIn.** The standard sign-in (OpenID Connect) returns only an ID, name, profile picture and email address. Work history and education are not available to regular apps. ([LinkedIn docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/consumer/integrations/self-serve/sign-in-with-linkedin-v2))
- **Instagram.** The Basic Display API, the only API for personal accounts, was shut down on 4 December 2024. The remaining Instagram API covers business and creator accounts only. ([Meta developer docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-basic-display-api/overview))
- **X.** The free tier is effectively write-only; reading posts is paid. Inferring interests from posts would also risk deriving political or religious views, which ADR-002 rules out.
- **Goodreads.** Has not issued new API keys since 8 December 2020. ([Goodreads developer forum](https://www.goodreads.com/topic/show/21788520-api-deprecation))

Sources checked on 16 October 2026.

## Options

1. **Platform APIs.** Blocked for LinkedIn career data, Instagram personal accounts and Goodreads; paid and privacy-risky for X.
2. **Scraping public profiles.** Breaks the platforms' terms of service, is fragile, and processes personal data without the user's real consent. Not acceptable for a product whose whole promise is trust between strangers.
3. **Declared data, plus data portability and open APIs.**
   - Users pick interests, books, schools and profession during onboarding.
   - Optionally, they upload their own data archive from Instagram, X or LinkedIn: an export that GDPR (Art. 20) entitles them to and that the platforms offer to all users. We extract only the fields we need and delete the archive.
   - Sign in with LinkedIn offers a quick login (LinkedIn notes it does not verify identity, so we don't present it as verification); Open Library and Google Books supply book metadata and covers.

## Decision

Option 3. Declared data is the primary source. The archive import is an optional accelerator that pre-fills onboarding, never a requirement.

## Consequences

- **Onboarding gets longer.** This is the main risk, so onboarding completion is a guardrail metric for the A/B test. The import exists to reduce typing for the users who want it.
- **Data quality is better.** What users choose to declare reflects how they want to be matched, not what an algorithm guessed from their likes.
- **No platform dependency.** A policy change at Meta, X or LinkedIn cannot break matching.
- **Archive parsing is real engineering work.** Each platform's export format differs and changes without notice. Parsers need versioned fixtures and must fail softly, falling back to manual entry.
- **Clear privacy story.** We never hold a platform token or a raw archive; the planned ADR-006 makes this a hard rule.
